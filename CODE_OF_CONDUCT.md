# ModelPort Community Conduct

ModelPort welcomes people with different backgrounds, identities and levels of experience. Participation should make technical work safer and easier to review, not impose a requirement to tolerate harassment.

This is an independently authored project policy. It applies to issues, pull requests, reviews, discussions and other spaces maintained for the project, and to conduct while representing the project elsewhere.

## Expectations

- Discuss code, evidence and tradeoffs rather than attacking a person.
- Give specific, constructive feedback and make room for questions and corrections.
- Respect a person's stated name, identity, boundaries and privacy.
- Be honest about authorship, licenses, test results and limitations of AI-assisted work.
- Protect credentials and private prompts, recordings, media and account information.
- Accept a reasonable request to stop a conversation or move it to an appropriate channel.

## Unacceptable Conduct

- Harassment, discriminatory abuse, threats, stalking or sexualized attention without consent.
- Publishing or threatening to publish private information without permission.
- Deliberately misleading contributors about security, cost, evidence or affiliation.
- Repeated personal attacks, intimidation, disruptive spam or retaliation for reporting a concern.
- Pressuring someone to disclose secrets, pay for tests, or expose private data to reproduce an issue.

## Reporting

The repository owner must publish a monitored private community-reporting contact before public launch. This source checkout does not invent an email address or claim that such a contact is already configured.

Use the private contact explicitly provided by the actual repository owner. If none is available, ask for a private reporting route without identifying affected people or disclosing incident details in a public issue. For a software vulnerability, follow [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md) instead.

Include the relevant context, links and requested protective measures, sharing only information necessary to understand the concern. Maintainers should limit disclosure to people needed to handle it, while avoiding promises of absolute confidentiality they cannot fulfill.

## Enforcement

Maintainers may remove content, ask for a correction, issue a warning, restrict participation temporarily, or remove access for serious or repeated violations. They should consider safety, context and patterns of behavior and explain decisions to affected participants when doing so is appropriate.

Retaliation is itself a violation. A participant may request reconsideration through the published private contact. Where possible, a maintainer not directly involved should review the request. This policy does not require a harmed person to confront another participant or continue interacting with them.
